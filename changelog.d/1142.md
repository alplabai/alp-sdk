### Added — a Linux devicetree claim on a CM33-owned RZ/V2N pad is now a CI failure (#1142)

V2N is an AMP part, and its two cores declared pads in two trees that never met:
the A55's claims live in the Linux devicetree under `meta-alp-sdk/`, the CM33's
in `metadata/pinmux/v2n.yaml`'s `core` field (#1157). Neither side could see the
other at build time. That is how the USB over-current `gpio-hog` on `P9.6`
silently clobbered the CM33's live `SCK7` mux at every Linux boot once the GD32
supervisor SPI took the pad — a `PMC9`/`PM9` byte-RMW landing at ~1.9 s, inside
the CM33's pin-setup window, recorded at `docs/errata-e1m-x-v2n.md:76`
("half of the hog REGRESSED"). A bench session found that; nothing in CI could.

`scripts/check_amp_pad_claims.py` closes the loop. Every `RZV2N_GPIO(...)` and
`RZV2N_PORT_PINMUX(...)` under `meta-alp-sdk/recipes-kernel/linux/` resolves to a
pad, and a pad the pinmux table attributes to the CM33 is now a hard error naming
the file, the line and the fix. The gate is validated by execution against the
known-bad tree, not by reading: the pre-2026-06-12 `P9.6` hog is replayed
verbatim in `tests/scripts/test_check_amp_pad_claims.py` and must go red.

The devicetree side of this issue was already correct in the tree and is
unchanged. The hog is `PB.1`-only, and the port-9 AMP rule is already written
above `usb-ovc-disable-hog` in
`meta-alp-sdk/recipes-kernel/linux/linux-renesas/e1m-x-evk.dtsi` — "P9.6 (usb20
OVC) is DELIBERATELY absent". What was missing was anything that enforced it.

**One documented exemption, and it is a finding, not a formality.** `BRD_I2C` /
`RIIC8` (`P06`/`P07`) is dual-master by design. The CM33 muxes it at
`zephyr/boards/alp/e1m_v2n101_m33_sm/alp_e1m_v2n101_m33_sm-pinctrl.dtsi:42-43`
("SDA P06"), and so does Linux, whose `i2c8_pins` group claims the same two pads
to drive the GD32 bridge expander through a purpose-built kernel driver
(`0005-gpio-add-gd32-bridge-expander-driver.patch`). The `core` field cannot say
"both": `m33` is the value it carries today, and an absent `core` is documented
as "not attributed", never "shared". So the metadata cannot state the truth about
these two pads either way, and the pair is exempt in one place with both claims
cited. Widening that vocabulary is a schema decision (#1157) left to the
maintainer. An exemption matching no `core: "m33"` row is itself a hard error, so
it cannot rot into decoration.
