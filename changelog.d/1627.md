### Fixed — `zephyr_drv`'s counter `close()` could leave a stale channel alarm armed (#1627)

`z_close()` stopped the counter but never cancelled the channel-0 alarm
`counter_stop()` does not guarantee cancellation of, so a still-armed alarm
from a closed handle could deliver into a recycled handle, or make a freshly
reopened handle's `alp_counter_set_alarm()` fail `ALP_ERR_BUSY` on the
native-sim counter driver.

`z_close()` now cancels the channel-0 alarm before releasing the slot. On the
canonical `alp_counter_stop()` + `alp_counter_close()` teardown order the
counter is already stopped by the time `close()` runs, and at least the
native-sim backend's `cancel_channel_alarm()` refuses (`-ENOTSUP`) on a
stopped counter without clearing the pending alarm — so if the first cancel
attempt fails, `z_close()` now briefly restarts the counter to let the cancel
take effect, then leaves it stopped as the caller left it.

`alp_counter_dispatch.c`'s `close()` also clears `alarm_cb`/`alarm_user` on
the handle independent of backend behaviour, and `alp_counter_set_alarm()`
now rolls those fields back to NULL when the backend rejects the arm request
(e.g. `ALP_ERR_BUSY`) instead of leaving a callback installed that a leaked
driver-side alarm could still reach.

`include/alp/counter.h` now documents that `alp_counter_close()` cancels any
armed alarm as part of teardown, and softens `alp_counter_set_alarm()`'s
replace-semantics claim to note it is backend-dependent.

Regression tests: `tests/unit/counter_registry/` gained
`test_zephyr_drv_close_cancels_channel_alarm` (close while still running) and
`test_zephyr_drv_close_cancels_channel_alarm_after_stop` (the
`alp_counter_stop()` + `alp_counter_close()` ordering) — both exercise the
real `zephyr_drv` backend against a `counter0` alias newly wired into the
`native_sim`/`native_sim/native/64` overlays.
