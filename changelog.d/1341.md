### Changed — ADR 0026 accepted, with its migration re-scoped: the deletion unit was wrong and would have removed a hardware-safety gate (#1341)

ADR 0026 ("Tan owns the planner. alp-sdk stops producing plans.") moves from
`Proposed` to `Accepted`. The decision, its five clauses, the measurement that
motivated them and the rejected alternatives all stand — reading both repos
before executing it strengthened the case rather than weakening it. What did not
survive review is the Migration section.

**Step 3 was not executable as written.** It said to delete
`scripts/alp_orchestrate/` "with `git grep` evidence that nothing references it".
That evidence cannot be produced: the directory is not the planner. It is
simultaneously the planner, the per-slice config emitters, and the `board.yaml`
loader the rest of the SDK imports as a library. `scripts/alp_project.py:179-186`
and `:224-230` import `load_board_yaml`, `emit_system_manifest`,
`emit_ipc_contract_h`, `emit_dts_reservations`, `emit_os_topology`,
`_slice_alp_conf`, `_slice_cmake_args` and `_slice_local_conf` from it;
`metadata/emit-registry-v1.json` names its modules as the `owner.module` for
roughly seventeen emit modes; at least seven `check_*.py` gates import it.

**And the deletion would have removed a hardware-safety gate.**
`SdkRevisionUnknown` / `SdkRevisionNotBuildable` / `SdkRevisionUnsupported` come
from `alp_orchestrate.models`, are raised at
`scripts/alp_project_loader.py:436-457`, and are imported by
`scripts/validate_board_yaml.py:21` and `scripts/gen_catalog.py:93,340,353-359`.
That chain is the SDK declining to build a hardware revision it does not support
rather than producing an image for it. Worse, `validate_board_yaml.py` is the
script **tan itself spawns** on the default `tan validate` path
(tan-cli `python/tan/commands/validate_cmd.py:319`, spawned at `:1497-1508`), so
a naive `rm -rf` would break tan's own default validate path after tan had become
the only CLI.

The amendment replaces steps 2-4 with a six-step sequence whose order is
load-bearing, inserting the split step the ADR was missing (separate the planner
axis from the emitter/loader core, keeping the revision gate) before any
deletion. It also records four things the Migration section never decided: who
answers the configure-time `alp_project.py --emit zephyr-conf --core <id>` call
that scaffolded and example `CMakeLists.txt` make (repointing it at `tan` would
make tan a hard dependency of every user project build, including a plain `west
build`, and reaches projects already shipped); who owns rendered-artefact bytes
now that clause 4 retires the parity axis without retiring the duplicated
renderers; where the surviving oracle checks live, since `parity-seam1.yml` diffs
the frozen oracle against a **live SDK emit** and is dead by construction once
the SDK cannot emit; and what `sdkVersion` / `sdkCommit` mean once tan is the
emitter.

Two measured facts also correct the ADR's own text. A cross-repo audit found that
of the 20 `--emit` modes, 19 are re-implemented in tan against alp-sdk's raw
metadata and one (`scaffold`) is a byte-for-byte vendored snapshot — none is
consumed as a schema-validated envelope, so the duplication clause 4 leaves
unpoliced is wider than the planner. And clause 4's second bullet, which
preserves "the frozen `crates/` oracle axis, until `crates/` is deleted on its own
schedule", is already stale: `tan-cli` at `b9aa697` has no top-level `crates/`.
Both implementations are Python, so no language-boundary argument for keeping two
remains.

Finally, the parity axis may be **deleted** rather than merely disabled only once
the second producer exists in no alp-sdk release that tan's supported-version
floor still accepts — until then, an old SDK with a new tan has two producers
again and no gate.

Docs only; no code, schema or metadata change.
