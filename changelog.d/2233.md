### Fixed — Flow D MRAM writes sector-pad neighbours and prove themselves with a fresh-session read-back, not `verifybin` (#2233)

Two defects measured on real hardware during the #2067 persistence proof made
every Flow D MRAM writer unsafe as written. SEGGER's built-in
`AE822FA0E5597LS0_M55_HE` loader always erases and reprograms WHOLE 16 KiB
sectors and never reads a sector's prior contents first, so a `loadbin` that
does not start and end on a sector boundary silently turned every other byte
in its first/last sector to `0xFF`. Separately, `verifybin` was comparing
against J-Link's own in-process flash cache, never a fresh chip read — a
`Verify successful.` proved nothing about what MRAM actually held.

`scripts/bench/aen/flowd_sector_pad.py:63` (`class FlowdSectorPadError(Exception):`)
is a new, pure host-side helper: given the blob(s) a write touches, it merges
their sector-aligned ranges, overlays the blob bytes on a pre-read sector
image, and refuses on a blob outside the MRAM window or two blobs overlapping
with different bytes. `scripts/bench/aen/bench-env.sh:1638`
(`bench_flowd_proof() {`) is the shared bash orchestration — plan, read the
touched sectors, build the padded images, and a FRESH read-only J-Link
session that `savebin`s each range back and `cmp`s it against the padded
image, which proves both that the blob landed and that its neighbours
survived.

`scripts/bench/aen/flash-jlink.sh:177` (`# GATE ON THE READ-BACK PROOF (#2233, replacing the old #1488 verifybin gate):`)
shows the shape every affected writer now follows — flash-jlink.sh,
flash-jlink-hp.sh, flash-jlink-mramxip.sh, flash-update-log-dual.sh,
flash-update-log-firewall-probe.sh, and the #1430 provisioning
erase-storage.sh all gate on `bench_flowd_proof()` instead of `verifybin`.
`FLOWD_DRY_RUN=1` exercises the whole pipeline (planned sectors, padded
ranges, the write and proof CommandFiles) without ever opening a probe.
