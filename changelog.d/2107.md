### Fixed — CC3501E reply-header gate is now sized by the reply capacity the caller offers, not a flat 200 us (#2107)

The host used to wait a fixed, blind 200 us (`cc3501e_reply_gate(ctx, 200u)`)
before clocking the CC3501E bridge's reply header on every request, regardless
of how much reply data that request expected back. That was proven fine for
small replies but too short for large ones: the bridge composes a large reply
(ring copy, a CRC over ~4100 bytes, a ~4100-byte memcpy) inside its SPI
transfer-complete ISR before it re-arms the reply-header phase, and that
composition cost exceeds 200 us well before the reply approaches
`ALP_CC3501E_MAX_PAYLOAD` (4096 B). A 4071 B `SOCK_RECV` wedged the link on the
very first call, 2/2 runs at both 1 MHz and 25 MHz, while a 512 B `SOCK_RECV`
was fine 3/3; widening only that gate to 2000 us made the same 4071 B
`SOCK_RECV` succeed 3/3, including a 262144 B download with a matching CRC.

`cc3501e_reply_header_gate_us()` (`chips/cc3501e/cc3501e_core.c`) replaces the
flat 200 us with a gate scaled by `rx_cap` -- the reply capacity the caller
passed into this specific request: the proven 200 us floor holds for `rx_cap`
at or below 512 B, and the gate grows linearly to the proven 2000 us at
`rx_cap == ALP_CC3501E_MAX_PAYLOAD` (4096 B). Only those two points (512 B at
200 us, 4071 B at 2000 us) were bench-measured; every `rx_cap` strictly between
them is linearly interpolated, not separately measured.

Per-op effect: every small-reply op in this driver (`PING`, `GET_VERSION`,
`GET_CAPABILITIES`, `GET_DIAG_INFO`, `DIAG_GET_STATS`, the BLE
enable/disable/advertise/connect/GATT-register family, `SOCK_OPEN`/`BIND`/
`LISTEN`/`SEND`/`CLOSE`, `GET_MAC`, `WIFI_GET_RSSI`/`GET_IP`/`STATUS`,
`STREAM_WRITE`, every `OTA_*` control opcode, `GPIO_CONFIGURE`/
`SET_INTERRUPT`, `POWER_POLICY`, and the camera control opcodes) requests a
reply well under 512 B and keeps the unchanged 200 us gate. The large-reply
ops -- `BLE_SCAN_START`, `WIFI_SCAN_START`, `GET_PENDING_EVENTS`, and
`SOCK_RECV` (each sized to the 4096 B `ALP_CC3501E_MAX_PAYLOAD` scratch buffer)
-- now gate at the proven 2000 us, an added ~1800 us of latency per request
that only these large-reply calls pay. `SPI1_TRANSFER`'s reply buffer
(4 + `ALP_CC3501E_SPI1_MAX_XFER`, 4092 B) falls just inside the interpolated
range and gates at ~1998 us.

Does not touch `CC3501E_PHASE_SETTLE_US` (the request-header, request-payload,
and reply-payload phases) or the READY-line edge-wait path -- both stay exactly
as they were; only the reply-header phase's blind-fallback computation changed.
