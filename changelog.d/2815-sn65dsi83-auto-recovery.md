### Fixed — the SN65DSI83 bridge recovers by itself after it resets to its defaults (panel black, backlight on) (#2815)

On the bench (E1M-AEN803 + Riverdi RVT121) the panel went black with the backlight on while the SoC side stayed healthy (CDC200 scanning, DSI PHY locked, EN `P13_4` high): the bridge had reset itself (CSR `0x0D` `0x00`, `0x0A` `0x0A`, `0xE5` `0x3D`; healthy reads `0x01`, `0x81`, `0x00`) and its init ran only once at boot. The driver now health-checks the bridge every `CONFIG_SN65DSI83_RECOVERY_INTERVAL_MS` (default 1000, `CONFIG_SN65DSI83_RECOVERY`, default y) and, when PLL_EN is clear, the PLL is not locked or `HS_CLK_SRC` reads back different from the table, replays the same init from PLL off (ID check, CSR table, PLL start, `0xE5` clear), rate-limited to one re-init per 5 s, counted and reported with `printk` and the register values (the apps keep `CONFIG_LOG` off). `0xE5` error bits with the PLL fine are cleared and counted, and re-init only when they persist on 3 polls in a row or show on the first poll after a re-init; a replay that fails part-way writes `0x0D` = `0x00` so the next window retries. `sn65dsi83_recovery_count()` returns the counter. The recipe is published right after the ID check, before the CSR bank is written, so a boot init that fails half-way is recoverable too. When the display core hands I2C1 to another core after init (the Trace Runner with `-DTR_INPUT_NPU=ON`: the HE configures the bridge, the HP owns the controller for the camera), the display core never touches the bus again: the driver publishes its CSR table in shared SRAM (`recovery-recipe-address`, `0x0237FE00`, `TR_MEM_SN65_RECIPE`) and an `alp,sn65dsi83-recovery` node on the bus-owning core replays it from that core's own I2C driver on its own low-priority work queue, so the check cannot race the camera's transfers; a build that hands the bridge's bus away without the recipe address fails at compile time. Replay into a live video stream is bench-unverified. The bus-owning core latches the first valid recipe in its own RAM, so a warm reboot of the display core (which clears the recipe magic and may lose the race for the bus it handed away) still leaves the bridge watched. An HE-only reset under the running HP is now a recognised warm boot (`alp,i2c-handover` `alive-address` = a liveness word `0x0237FD90` the HP advances from a 10 ms timer started right after it took the bus: record taken (snapshotted at PRE_KERNEL_1, where an untaken release is also cleared) and the word moving, sampled up to 100 ms): the HE does not initialise I2C1 (`zephyr,deferred-init` on its `&i2c1`, `device_init()` on a cold boot only), keeps the bridge EN pin high, does not touch the bridge or release the bus, keeps the recipe magic and counters and republishes the recipe, and re-initialises only the DSI host and the CDC200; the HP agent replays the CSRs if the host restart cost the bridge its PLL lock, rides out I2C errors (retry at the next interval, one report per episode of 3 failing polls in a row, never touching the shared controller). Bench-unverified. The Trace Runner HE also asks the Secure Enclave to reset the SoC after a fatal error (`TR_HE_FAULT_SOC_RESET`, default ON: a bounded direct MHUv2 request, not the mutex-guarded `se_service_boot_reset_soc()`), unless the fatal errors repeat within 30 s of each boot (3rd in a row halts, `TR_MEM_HE_RESET_GUARD`); `TR_BENCH_FAULT_INJECT` (default ON, inert until armed over SWD at `TR_MEM_FAULT_INJECT`) lets the bench test that. The Trace Runner HE also keeps its last fatal error (reason, PC, LR, CFSR, HFSR, MMFAR, BFAR) and the tail of its console in the shared SRAM0 page (`TR_MEM_HE_FAULT`, `TR_HE_FAULT_LOG`, default ON) and prints them at the next boot.
