### Fixed — scope the OSPI0 NOR/HyperRAM identity, population, and chip-select claims to the SKU each is actually true for (#2062)

`git grep -n 'R2 BOM'`, `git grep -n MX25UM25645`, and `git grep -n
W958D8NB` against `origin/dev` found sites stating in the present tense
that the E1M-AEN OSPI0 NOR is a Macronix `MX25UM25645GXDI00` populated
(`DNP = 0`) on "the R2 BOM," that its HyperRAM sibling is a Winbond
`W958D8NBYA5I`, or both. Measured contradiction (issue #2041): on
`E1M-AEN803` serial `2026W36-0002`, Flow C ITCM RAM-run, the NOR answered a
JEDEC ID read on OSPI0 **CS1** with `9d 5b 19 10` at `BAUDR=20` (20 MHz),
repeated on the alternate ISSI ReadID opcode `0x9E`. `0x9d` is the ISSI
manufacturer ID; `5b 19` is the `IS25WX256` type/capacity pair -- matching
`metadata/e1m_modules/E1M-AEN803.yaml:59-72`'s `IS25WX256-JHLE` NOR and
`:75-94`'s `S80KS5122GABHM02` (Infineon/Cypress) HyperRAM. Macronix's
manufacturer ID is `0xc2`.

A second review round found the greppable part-name sites were not the
whole defect: `scripts/gen_zephyr_board.py` could still print a population
claim contradicting its own preset (a generated E1M-AEN803 board said
"populated" in its banner and "not populated on this batch" two paragraphs
later, from two separately-hardcoded sentences), and several population
claims the part-name greps cannot reach -- `dram_mbit`/`flash_mbit` values,
a preset's own cross-reference to its sibling SKU -- were left standing.
Both rounds are folded into one list below rather than kept as two: the
list below is the recount, **25 distinct sites across 17 hand-edited
files**, plus 2 regenerated `.dts` files -- not the "Five sites" an
earlier written-list draft of this fragment claimed, nor the "fifteen
sites across thirteen files" a first, still-incomplete recount claimed
after review round 2 alone.

Four separate errors were mixed into these sites and are corrected
independently:

1. **Part identity.** Macronix `MX25UM25645GXDI00` -> measured ISSI
   `IS25WX256-JHLE`; Winbond `W958D8NBYA5I` -> measured
   `S80KS5122GABHM02` (Infineon/Cypress) -- cited to #2041, everywhere the
   claim named a SKU/footprint that actually populates OSPI0.
2. **Population.** `metadata/e1m_modules/E1M-AEN801.yaml:60-88` declares
   *both* external memories `assembled: false` (`memory.dram_mbit: 0`,
   `memory.flash_mbit: 0` at `:155-156`) -- E1M-AEN801 runs from the SoC's
   on-die MRAM and populates neither device on any revision as this repo
   models it. "The R2 BOM populates only OSPI0" is false for that SKU;
   every corrected site now says which SKU (E1M-AEN803) the claim is
   actually true for.
3. **Chip select.** Several sites placed the NOR on CS0. The netlist puts
   `U10` (NOR) on `OSPI0_SS1` and `U9` (HyperRAM) on `OSPI0_SS0`; metadata
   was corrected to match in `44200cab` (#1944, #1990, PR #2007); #2041
   confirmed it by distinguishing three `ser` values -- `0x00000002` (CS1)
   drains `9d 5b 19 10`, `0x00000001` (CS0) drains `00 00 00 00`,
   `0x00000000` drains 0/4 with `SR = 0x00000002`.
4. **HyperRAM capacity.** `metadata/e1m_modules/E1M-AEN803.yaml:92`
   measures the fitted HyperRAM at `512` Mbit (64 MiB), not the `256` Mbit
   the Winbond part's own datasheet capacity would have implied --
   `S80KS5122GABHM02` is a larger part than the Winbond one it replaces.

**Why AEN801 keeps ISSI/Infineon-Cypress part names on an unpopulated
footprint (scope decision, corrected reasoning from an earlier draft of
this fragment):** E1M-AEN801 and E1M-AEN803 are the same SoC
(`AE822FA0E5597LS0`) on the same PCB, and E1M-AEN803 is E1M-AEN801's
*populated BOM variant* -- not a different design, the same U10/U9
footprint stuffed. Its measured parts are therefore the parts that
footprint takes on this SoC, whether or not any individual E1M-AEN801 unit
carries them. E1M-AEN{301,401,501,601,701} are **different SoCs** (E3-E7,
not E8) with no populated BOM variant ever measured -- the 24-ball OSPI
footprint they share with AEN801/803 accepts several different vendors'
parts, and which one a stuffed unit of those SKUs would carry is a real
open BOM question, not a PCB fact the way AEN801/AEN803's shared-SoC case
is. An earlier draft of this fragment justified leaving those five SKUs'
`chip:` fields alone with reasoning ("the 24-ball footprint accepts
several MPNs") that, read literally, applied equally to AEN801 and would
have argued against this fragment's own AEN801 fix -- corrected above.
