### Fixed — CC3501E power presets never configured the radio (#1679)

`CMD_POWER_POLICY` (0x62), its four presets and the host driver have existed for a
while, and the MCU core half was a real implementation. But nothing in
`firmware/cc3501e/` ever called `Wlan_Set()`, so the Wi-Fi radio stayed fully awake
under every preset — DEEP_SLEEP included. On a Wi-Fi part that is the dominant
term: an associated station that never enters power save keeps its receiver up
continuously, which costs far more than any core idle state saves. In practice the
four presets differed only in core state, which is why the code documented
LOW_POWER and DEEP_SLEEP as sharing one reachable state.

The presets now drive the radio too, via the three knobs `wlan_if.h` exposes:
`WLAN_SET_POWER_SAVE` (active / auto / power-save), `WLAN_SET_POWER_MANAGEMENT`
(always-active / ELP) and `WLAN_SET_LSI` (wake every DTIM vs every Nth DTIM).
LSI is what makes LOW_POWER and DEEP_SLEEP genuinely different, and it gives
`idle_ms_before_sleep` a meaning — that field was previously accepted and discarded
because the vendor Power driver exposes no idle-hysteresis setter.

`Wlan_Set()` is rejected while the radio is down, so the policy is latched and
re-applied once `Wlan_RoleUp(STA)` succeeds; without that, a policy set before
Wi-Fi came up was silently dropped.

**Trade-off.** Waking only every Nth DTIM queues inbound frames at the AP until the
next wake, so DEEP_SLEEP adds hundreds of ms of inbound latency and will cut bulk
throughput hard. That is the preset working as intended, and it is why BALANCED
remains the default.

Also corrects the public header, which described DEEP_SLEEP as "radios off;
wake-on-host only". That was never what the firmware did and still is not — the
station stays associated in every preset.
