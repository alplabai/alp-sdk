### Changed — the AEN ADC driver now says out loud that `ADC_CLK` is out of spec, instead of reading silently (#1823)

Every ADC conversion on E8 runs the SAR roughly twice as fast as the part is
characterised for, and nothing said so. HWRM Table 19-2 sources `ADC_CLK` from
`PERIPH_CLK`, and 19.1.5.3.4 `ADC_CLK_DIVISOR` accepts only `0x2`..`0x10`
(divide by 2..16). `PERIPH_CLK` is bench-measured at ~156.5 MHz on `AE822` —
twice independently, from ADC conversion timing over 2000 reads and from the
SE's own boot line `[SES] SE frequency is 78.31 MHz`, exactly half. So even the
maximum divisor 16 gives ~9.8 MHz against the `ADTS0013` v1.2 maximum of
**5000 kHz**. The DT nodes already carry `clock_div = <16>`, the lowest
`ADC_CLK` reachable
(`zephyr/dts/alif/ensemble_e8_peripherals.dtsi:1315` ("lowest ADC_CLK reachable")).

`adc_alif.c` now emits one `LOG_WRN` per instance at init naming the datasheet
limit and stating that its ENOB and linearity figures do not apply to these
readings
(`zephyr/drivers/adc/adc_alif.c:1219` ("ADC_CLK is out of spec")).

It warns rather than clamping or refusing, deliberately: there is no in-spec
setting to clamp **to**, and refusing would break every working ADC caller for a
limitation the hardware cannot avoid. Conversions complete and the readings are
usable — this is an accuracy caveat, not a failure. What changes is that a
caller can now see it, instead of trusting a number whose stated accuracy does
not hold.

This does not close #1823. Resolving it needs Alif: either a `PERIPH_CLK`
prescaler that is not in `AHRM0012NDA` v0.3, or a divisor above 16 with a
constraint the manual omits. Until then the warning is the honest state.
