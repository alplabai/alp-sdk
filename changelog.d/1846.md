### Fixed — TAS2563 driver accepted its own write-only broadcast address, which no entry point can ever reach (#1846)

Every I2C entry point in the TAS2563 driver reads: `tas2563_init()`'s
connectivity probe reads the revision register, `tas2563_read_revision()`
reads it directly, and `tas2563_set_mode()` is a read-modify-write of the
mode-control register (`tas2563_set_hw_enable()` is GPIO-only, no bus
traffic). `TAS2563_I2C_ADDR_BROADCAST` is the chip's global broadcast
address and is write-only on the part, so it could not have succeeded at
any of those entry points on any board — the driver's address validation
accepted it anyway, and `tas2563_init()` would run its write-then-read
probe (`select_page()` write, then a `TAS2563_REG_REVID` read) against it
and fail with `ALP_ERR_NOT_READY` at best, or land on whatever other
device happens to answer at that address on the given board.

`addr_is_valid()` in `chips/tas2563/tas2563.c` now accepts only the four
AD0/SPICLK strap addresses; `TAS2563_I2C_ADDR_BROADCAST` is rejected with
`ALP_ERR_INVAL`. The `#define` stays in `include/alp/chips/tas2563.h` —
it's a real datasheet address and the one a future write-only broadcast
op would target — with a doc comment explaining why `tas2563_init()`
won't take it today.

On the E1M-X EVK this closes a concrete hazard: INA236B (U32,
`+VCAM2` rail) is strapped to `0x48`, the same address as the TAS2563
broadcast address (`metadata/boards/e1m-x-evk.yaml`), and the driver's
old `select_page()` write during init would have landed on INA236's
CONFIG register instead. That collision was the trigger for filing this,
but it isn't the whole defect — the SDK-side fix here doesn't know or
care what else is on the bus; it just refuses an address this driver can
never legitimately use. The board-level collision itself is unchanged
by this fix and is documented in `docs/boards/e1m-x-evk.md`, matching
the existing note for the identical U32 collision on the E1M-EVK
(`docs/boards/e1m-evk.md`) — only a hardware respin re-strapping U32's
A0 off `0x48` restores broadcast addressing on this board.
