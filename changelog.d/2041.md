### Fixed — OSPI0 pinctrl was never applied by the AEN driver, and its regcheck example muxed the wrong board's pads (#2041)

`zephyr/drivers/flash/flash_ospi_alif.c` declared `pinctrl-0`/`pinctrl-names`
as required in its DT binding
(`zephyr/dts/bindings/ospi/snps,designware-ospi.yaml:37-41`, already present
before this change) but never applied them: `grep -ci pinctrl` over the
driver returned `0`. Every consumer had to mux the OSPI0 pads itself or
silently get an unmuxed bus. The driver now includes
`<zephyr/drivers/pinctrl.h>` under `CONFIG_PINCTRL`
(`flash_ospi_alif.c:359-361`), carries a `pinctrl_dev_config *pcfg` in
`struct ospi_alif_config` (`:376-378`), and calls
`pinctrl_apply_state(config->pcfg, PINCTRL_STATE_DEFAULT)` in
`ospi_alif_init()` before the clock-enable and before
`alif_hal_ospi_initialize()` (`:445-462`), with `PINCTRL_DT_INST_DEFINE`
and `.pcfg = PINCTRL_DT_INST_DEV_CONFIG_GET(inst)` wired into the
`OSPI_ALIF_INIT` instantiation macro (`:521`, `:533`). The pattern —
`#ifdef CONFIG_PINCTRL` include, apply-at-top-of-init, `IF_ENABLED`-gated
`PINCTRL_DT_INST_DEFINE`/`.pcfg` — is copied from the sibling
`zephyr/drivers/spi/spi_dw_alif.c`'s `spi_dw_init()`
(`spi_dw_alif.c:1569-1571`) and its `pcfg` field
(`spi_dw_alif.h:79`). **This driver change is unverified reasoning: it has
never been compiled or run.**

`examples/aen/aen-ospi-regcheck`'s overlay muxed the Alif DevKit's own
OSPI0 route (`PIN_P3_0__OSPI0_SCLK_B`, `PIN_P3_2__OSPI0_SS0_B`,
`P2_0..P2_7`), a pad set the E1M-AEN module does not carry at all. It now
muxes the module's actual routing per
`metadata/e1m_modules/aen/alif-ospi.tsv` — 13 pads on ports 0/2/6/12, all
function 1 —
(`examples/aen/aen-ospi-regcheck/boards/alp_e1m_aen801_m55_he_ae822fa0e5597ls0_rtss_he.overlay:52-64`).

The example's header, its board overlay header, the SoC dtsi node's own
comment, and its `CMakeLists.txt`/`prj.conf`/`testcase.yaml` all asserted
"no octal-NOR/HyperBus part is populated" and that a live XiP read was
HW-blocked for that reason. **That premise is silicon-measured wrong.**
OSPI0 CS1 (SS1) on `E1M-AEN803`, serial `2026W36-0002`, answers a JEDEC ID
read (opcode `0x9F`, repeating on the alternate ISSI opcode `0x9E`) with
`9d 5b 19 10` — `0x9d` = ISSI, `5b 19` = the IS25WX256 type/capacity pair,
i.e. a populated ISSI `IS25WX256-JHLE`, not the Macronix
`MX25UM25645GXDI00` the header had assumed. Three mutually distinguishable
`ser` columns rule out the wrong-chip-select failure mode by measurement:
`ser = 0x00000002` (CS1/NOR) drained 4/4 bytes of that ID; `ser =
0x00000001` (CS0/HyperRAM) drained 4/4 bytes of `00 00 00 00`; `ser =
0x00000000` drained 0/4 with `SR = 0x00000002`. That measurement was NOT
taken through this example, which performs zero device-level transfers —
controller-register reads only (DT bind check, CTRLR0 readback) — so "the
OSPI memories are silent" was never actually tested at the device level by
anything in this repo; the corrected header says so explicitly
(`examples/aen/aen-ospi-regcheck/src/main.c:40-77,279-291`,
`.../boards/alp_e1m_aen801_m55_he_ae822fa0e5597ls0_rtss_he.overlay:11-32`,
`zephyr/dts/alif/ensemble_e8_peripherals.dtsi:761-772`,
`examples/aen/aen-ospi-regcheck/{CMakeLists.txt:8-13,prj.conf:11-14,testcase.yaml:6-7,18-19}`).
The XiP skip itself is unaffected and stands on its own die-level reason —
`OSPI_XIP_SER` does not exist on AE822 (`SOC_FEAT_OSPI_HAS_XIP_SER=0`) —
independent of what's populated.

`metadata/e1m_modules/E1M-AEN801.yaml`'s chip-select mapping already
matches this measurement: NOR is `chip_select: 1` == SS1
(`metadata/e1m_modules/E1M-AEN801.yaml:69`) and HyperRAM is
`chip_select: 0` == SS0 (`:88`). That correction landed in #2036
(2026-09-10), before this branch was cut from `dev` — it is verified here,
not re-changed, and the same mapping already holds on every other AEN SKU
(`E1M-AEN301/401/501/601/701/803.yaml`). Nothing here says anything about
the HyperRAM at CS0 beyond what was already known, and clock headroom
stays open: at `BAUDR=4` (100 MHz, the driver's DT-derived default) the
same CS1 read returns `4e ad 8c 88` — 4/4 drained, `RISR=0x00000001`, a
device answering with a sampling error, not silence — against
`BAUDR=20`'s (20 MHz) clean `9d 5b 19 10`. Two points is not a sweep, and
the default `BAUDR` is unchanged by this fix.
