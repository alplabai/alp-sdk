### Fixed — `wifi ap-stop` reported a working teardown as a flat failure (#1553)

`cmd_companion_wifi_ap_stop()` printed `ap stop failed (-4)` whenever
`cc3501e_wifi_ap_stop()` did not return `ALP_OK`. Against CC3501E firmware
protocol v4 that is the **expected** outcome even when the teardown worked:
`WIFI_AP_STOP` sends no acknowledgement the driver can confirm on, so
`poll_by_repeat` exhausts its down-window and returns `ALP_ERR_TIMEOUT` — the
same missing-confirmation gap `wifi ap` already documents for `WIFI_AP_START`
(#1385), which is why `ap start` says "unconfirmed" rather than "failed".

**Bench-measured on E1M-AEN801, 2026-08-18**, from a second independent radio
(the host's Intel AX200 via `netsh wlan show networks`, validated against known
APs before being trusted):

| step | observed |
|---|---|
| link health | `CC3501E protocol v4` — healthy, not wedged |
| soft-AP visible to the second radio before the stop | yes |
| `alp companion wifi ap-stop` | `ap stop failed (-4)` |
| polled after | visible at 0.1 s / 15.2 s / 30.3 s, **gone at 45.4 s** |

The teardown happens; only the confirmation is missing. (The 30–45 s latency is
the observing host's scan cache, not necessarily the radio's.) So "failed" was
actively misleading — an operator could not tell a teardown that did not happen
from one that did, and the flat failure pushed them toward the wrong
conclusion.

The console now says `ap stop unconfirmed (-4)`, names the missing
acknowledgement, and tells the operator to confirm out of band, mirroring
`ap start`. It still returns an error: one measurement does not make
`ALP_ERR_TIMEOUT` a success code, and the status still covers a busy bridge or
a transport fault during the radio-down window, so it stays *inconclusive*
rather than becoming *success*.

`cc3501e_wifi_ap_stop()`'s declaration is corrected to match. It claimed
"`ALP_OK` once the firmware acknowledged the AP stop", implying an
acknowledgement protocol v4 does not send; it now carries the same
`@warning` shape as `cc3501e_wifi_ap_start()`, records the measurement, and
states that `ALP_OK` is not currently reachable.

Two further findings from the same bench run, recorded on #1553 rather than
changed here:

- **`ap start` genuinely starts the AP despite its own `-4`** — the SSID was
  confirmed broadcasting on the independent radio. #1385's "unconfirmed"
  wording is accurate, not pessimistic.
- **A second `ap start` while an AP is already running does nothing.** A fresh
  SSID never appeared across 90 s of polling while the first AP was up,
  consistent with the CC35 already holding the AP role.
