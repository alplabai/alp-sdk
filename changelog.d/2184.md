### Fixed — a double-delimited changelog anchor silently downgraded to range-checked-only instead of failing (#2184)

`scripts/check_changelog_citations.py`'s `_ANCHOR` regex could not parse an
anchor written with **both** delimiters at once — `` (`"text"`) `` or
`` ("`text`") ``. The opening character class consumes one delimiter, and the
capture group then refuses the very next character because it is the OTHER
delimiter, so the whole match fails at position zero. The citation fell
through to the plain "no anchor" path and was graded as an ordinary
un-anchored, range-checked-only citation — silently, with no error, no skip,
and no line in the summary distinguishing it from a citation that was never
anchored to begin with. `--fix` inherited the same blind spot for the same
reason, so it could not repair what it could not see either.

That silent downgrade, not the malformed syntax itself, is this gate's third
blind spot found by hand rather than by the gate (after bare unbackticked
`path:NNN` and the empty-`changelog.d/` early return, #2178/#2185) — and it
is the worst of the three, because a double-delimited anchor *reads* as
anchored and text-verified to the next person, while the gate silently
treated it as though it carried no anchor at all.

Fixed by detecting the clash directly rather than requiring a symmetric
close: `_MALFORMED_ANCHOR` matches two delimiter characters back to back
right after the opening paren
(`scripts/check_changelog_citations.py:493`
(`malformed = _MALFORMED_ANCHOR.match(rest)`)), which is what actually breaks
`_ANCHOR` regardless of how the anchor text is shaped further in — the two
real instances on `dev` were not the tidy textbook shape either
(`` (`"i2s": 10`) `` closes on a bare backtick, and one anchor's own text
contained further backticks, which `_ANCHOR`'s capture class forbids on its
own). Both `_check_one` and `_fix_one` now report this as a hard ERROR
naming the two clashing delimiter characters, never a silent pass and never
folded into the ordinary "un-anchored" count `_fix_one` uses for a citation
that genuinely carries no anchor at all — a double-delimited anchor is
entirely fixable by its author right here, unlike a foreign-repo path or a
folded fragment, so it does not get the SKIP treatment those get either.

Known limitation left in place, not fixed here: `_MALFORMED_ANCHOR` only
catches a delimiter as the very first character after the opening paren, so
a "shortened, delimiter-free" anchor that still contains a delimiter later
in the span — `` (`ab"cd`) ``, `` (`: 1`) ``, `` (` "text") ``,
`` (`i2s"`) `` — is not this class at all. `_ANCHOR`'s own capture-refusal
class trips on it first instead, so it fails to match, falls through to "no
anchor", and degrades to range-checked-only exactly as silently as the
double-delimiter case this fix closes, with no error and no line in the
summary distinguishing it. Widening the detector past offset 0 is
deliberately out of scope here and is being filed separately.

Four live instances were found on `dev` this way, not the three expected —
`changelog.d/1171.md`, `changelog.d/2138.md`, `changelog.d/2139.md` and
`changelog.d/2149.md` (the last already carried a single-delimiter fix on
the unmerged #2190 branch, but `dev` itself still had the double-delimited
form). All four are repointed to a single delimiter in this change; the
anchored count over the whole tree rises by exactly 4 and the range-checked
count falls by the same 4.

Also carries the two nits deferred from the #2185 review of this same file:
`_fix_one`'s docstring now lists `_CHANGELOG_D_PREFIX` alongside `_CITATION`,
`_ANCHOR` and `_strip_fenced_blocks` as machinery it shares with `_check_one`
— it was the one condition duplicated by hand into both halves and the
docstring didn't say so — and the mutation-testing comment above `main()`'s
`--fix` placement now says the suite was 33 (its current size), not the
stale 29 left over from before #2185.
