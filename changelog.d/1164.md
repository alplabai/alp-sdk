### Fixed — the OPTIGA Trust M driver no longer points the next implementer at an upstream Zephyr module that does not exist (#1164)

The Trust M driver has been probe-only since v0.3, and both files that explain
why named the wrong next step. `chips/optiga_trust_m/optiga_trust_m.c` said the
answer was to "vendor the upstream library as a Zephyr module" and sized it at
"~5 KLOC". Neither holds. Infineon's `optiga-trust-m` at `release-v5.8.1` ships
no `zephyr/module.yml` and no library-level CMakeLists, so a west project pin
would be inert for the same reason `vendors/u8g2/README.md:9` ("ships no")
already documents; and its `src/` plus `include/` are 19,777 lines, not five
thousand. The comment now states what consuming it actually costs — vendored
source plus module glue alp-sdk writes itself — and names the two hardware gaps
that bound the work as much as the vendoring does
(`chips/optiga_trust_m/optiga_trust_m.c:14-27` ("ship is a Zephyr")).

The public header was wrong about the hardware.
`include/alp/chips/optiga_trust_m.h` told the reader the chip "sits on Alif's
LPI2C bus" on E1M-AEN, naming the one family where it is unreachable and
omitting the two where it is populated. AEN's BRD_I2C was believed at the time
to be LPI2C0, where the Alif silicon can only be a slave --
**since corrected by #1848: BRD_I2C is SoC I2C0, master-capable, not LPI2C0**
(`docs/bring-up-aen.md:208` ("so the M55 can master BRD_I2C directly")) -- and
the part is marked `DNI` on the current batch
(`docs/bring-up-aen.md:249` ("DNI on this bench batch")). The chip is populated
on E1M-X V2N / V2M at `0x30` on BRD_I2C with the RZ/V2N as bus master
(`metadata/e1m_modules/E1M-V2N101.yaml:45` ("role: secure_element")). The header
now says so
(`include/alp/chips/optiga_trust_m.h:20-26` ("Populated on E1M-X V2N / V2M")).

Two facts were verified rather than assumed while establishing this, and are
recorded so the vendoring decision can be made without repeating the work.
Licensing is not the blocker: all 57 files under upstream's `src/`, `include/`,
`config/` and `extras/pal/zephyr/` carry a per-file MIT SPDX identifier, and
`MIT` is already on this repo's allowlist at
`metadata/schemas/library-v1.schema.json:35` ("BSD-2-Clause"). And the existing
probe is correct: the register numbers and the four-byte `I2C_STATE` read in
`optiga_trust_m_init()` match upstream's own physical layer, so the probe stays
valid under a real transport.

The in-tree minimal-APDU alternative was costed too, separately from the
vendoring path above. #1164 names two ways off the stub: vendoring Infineon's
host library, costed above, or "an in-tree minimal APDU implementation of the
handful of commands actually needed" — never costed until now. Upstream's
`ifx_i2c_data_link_layer.c` (608 lines, an 11-state retry/resend/ack/nack
machine plus a CRC16 over every frame) and `ifx_i2c_transport_layer.c` (479
lines, cross-frame APDU chaining) are both required before
OpenApplication/CloseApplication (APDU commands `0x70`/`0x71` in upstream's
`src/cmd/optiga_cmd.c`, lines 31 and 33) or any other command APDU can run:
1,087 lines at minimum, hand-derived from the wire spec rather than run against
upstream's tested implementation, for a security element with no silicon in
this dispatch to verify it against even once. `ifx_i2c_presentation_layer.c`
(1086 lines) is not part of that minimum — its entire body is guarded
`#ifdef OPTIGA_COMMS_SHIELDED_CONNECTION`, the optional Shielded Connection
encryption layer, verified against a fresh clone of `release-v5.8.1`.
Vendoring the whole `src/comms/ifx_i2c/` directory instead — the three files
above plus `ifx_i2c_physical_layer.c` (720), `ifx_i2c.c` (315) and
`ifx_i2c_config.c` (140) — totals 3,348 lines, already tested against real
silicon. That cost, and the decision to defer both options rather than land
either blind, is recorded as this driver's own call at
`chips/optiga_trust_m/optiga_trust_m.c:29-55` — not attributed to #1164's
comment thread, which says the transport is desk-work-feasible and leaves
speculative-vs-wait as an open question, not a ruling.

The callers/tests that pin the current `NOSUPPORT` contract are recorded next
to it, so a future transport change moves them all in the same slice:
`tests/zephyr/chips/src/test_security.c:88` and
`tests/zephyr/chips/src/test_security.c:104-106`;
`examples/v2n/v2n-secure-element-sign/src/main.c:52` and
`examples/v2n/v2n-secure-element-sign/src/main.c:73`;
`examples/aen/aen-secure-element-sign/src/main.c:103` and
`examples/aen/aen-secure-element-sign/src/main.c:123`;
`docs/tutorials/06-secure-element-sign.md:23-30`.

No behaviour changed. `optiga_trust_m_send_apdu()` and
`optiga_trust_m_read_product_info()` still return `ALP_ERR_NOSUPPORT`, and
`metadata/chips/optiga_trust_m.yaml:48` ("untested") still records that not even
the probe is silicon-proven.
